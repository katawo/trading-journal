# Three-Pillar Trading Performance Framework

**Psychology • Risk Management • Trading System**

A simple framework for assessing trading quality, protecting capital, validating an edge, and improving the trader over time.

> **Framework thesis**
>
> Trading System creates the opportunity. Risk Management controls the cost of being wrong. Psychology determines how well the trader executes both.

---

# 1. Framework Overview

The framework separates trading performance into three distinct but connected pillars. Each pillar answers a different question and should be measured independently.

| Pillar | Simple question | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Psychology | Did I execute myself correctly? | Control behavior and follow the plan. |
| Risk Management | Did I manage risk correctly? | Protect capital and control exposure. |
| Trading System | Did I execute a valid system correctly? | Define, execute, and validate the trading edge. |

> **Core rule**
>
> A trade can make money and still be a bad trade. A trade can lose money and still be a good trade. **Outcome and process quality must be measured separately.**

## Framework lifecycle

The framework is primarily a **post-trade assessment and development system**.

**Core lifecycle:**

`Execute → Assess → Score → Review → Diagnose → Improve`

| Stage | Required? | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Pre-Trade Check | Optional | Prepare, check readiness, validate risk or setup before entry. |
| In-Trade Observation | Optional / light | Capture meaningful deviations during execution. |
| Post-Trade Assessment | **Required** | Assess Psychology, Risk Management, and Trading System execution. |
| Period Review | **Required** | Identify patterns across trades. |
| Improvement | **Required** | Define focused corrective actions and test changes. |

Pre-trade checks may be enabled when useful, but they are **not required for the framework to function**. The primary source of truth is what actually happened during and after the trade.

# 2. Pillar One — Psychology

> **Objective**
>
> Execute the trading process consistently under uncertainty without emotion, impulse, or bias overriding the plan.

## Six simple components

| Component | Simple question |
|---|---|
| 1. Readiness | Was my mental state suitable for trading? |
| 2. Patience | Did I wait for a valid opportunity? |
| 3. Discipline | Did I follow the rules when tempted to break them? |
| 4. Emotional Control | Did emotion change my decisions? |
| 5. Loss Acceptance | Did I accept normal losses without reacting badly? |
| 6. Recovery | Did I reset correctly after mistakes, losses, or emotional events? |

## Post-trade psychology assessment

- [ ] I traded for a valid reason, not boredom or impulse.
- [ ] I did not chase price.
- [ ] I did not revenge trade.
- [ ] I did not increase size emotionally.
- [ ] I followed the planned execution rules.
- [ ] I accepted the outcome without forcing another trade.
- [ ] I identified any emotional trigger or behavioral mistake.
- [ ] I recorded the lesson or corrective action when needed.

## Optional pre-trade psychology check

When enabled, keep it short:

- [ ] I am mentally fit to trade.
- [ ] I am not trying to recover previous losses.
- [ ] I can accept the planned risk.
- [ ] I am willing to wait for my setup.

## Hard psychology rules

- No revenge trading.
- No intentional emotional increase in size.
- No deliberate rule-breaking to recover losses.
- If a predefined mental-fitness stop condition exists, respect it.

## Psychology KPIs

| KPI | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rule Adherence % | How consistently behavioral and execution rules are followed. |
| Unplanned Trade % | How often impulse creates trades. |
| FOMO / Chase Rate | Frequency of emotionally driven entries. |
| Revenge Trade Count | Frequency of post-loss emotional trades. |
| Emotional Interference Rate | How often emotion changes entry, stop, exit, or sizing. |
| Critical Violations | Serious behavioral failures. |
| Psychology Score | Overall quality of trading behavior. |

---

# 3. Pillar Two — Risk Management

> **Objective**
>
> Keep losses controlled, preserve capital, and ensure that individual trades, losing streaks, or bad periods cannot cause unacceptable damage.

## Six simple layers

| Layer | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 1. Risk per Trade | Limit the damage from one idea. |
| 2. Position Size | Convert allowed risk into the correct size. |
| 3. Daily / Weekly Limits | Stop a bad period from becoming a disaster. |
| 4. Open Exposure | Control total and correlated risk. |
| 5. Drawdown Protocol | Reduce risk when performance deteriorates. |
| 6. Recovery / Scale Rules | Define how risk is reduced and restored. |

## Post-trade risk assessment

- [ ] Planned risk was known or reconstructable.
- [ ] Actual risk stayed within policy.
- [ ] Position size matched the intended risk.
- [ ] Stop / invalidation was respected.
- [ ] Stop was not widened simply to avoid realizing a loss.
- [ ] Daily / weekly risk limits were respected.
- [ ] Open exposure remained within policy.
- [ ] No recovery sizing or emotional oversizing occurred.

## Optional pre-trade risk check

When enabled:

- [ ] Invalidation / stop is defined.
- [ ] Risk per trade is within policy.
- [ ] Position size is calculated from risk and stop distance.
- [ ] Daily / weekly risk capacity remains available.
- [ ] Open exposure is within policy.

> **Use R as the common unit**
>
> **1R** is the amount planned to be lost if a trade reaches its valid stop. R allows results to be compared across instruments, account sizes, and time periods.

## Hard risk rules

- Do not exceed the maximum allowed risk without an explicitly defined exception policy.
- Respect daily / weekly shutdown rules.
- Do not widen a stop solely to avoid taking a planned loss.
- Do not increase size emotionally after losses.
- Respect drawdown and exposure limits.

## Risk KPIs

| KPI | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planned vs Actual Risk | Whether execution matched the risk plan. |
| Risk Policy Adherence % | Percentage of trades within policy. |
| Risk-Limit Breaches | Number of hard risk failures. |
| Average Loss R | How well losses are contained. |
| Max Daily Loss R | Worst daily damage. |
| Maximum Drawdown | Peak-to-trough stress on capital. |
| Open Risk / Exposure | Aggregate risk being carried. |
| Stop Discipline % | Whether stop rules were respected. |
| Risk Score | Overall quality of risk execution. |

# 4. Pillar Three — Trading System

> **Objective**
>
> Define and execute a repeatable trading process, then use evidence to determine whether the system has a genuine edge.

## Seven simple parts

| Part | Simple question |
|---|---|
| 1. Market | What do I trade? |
| 2. Context | Under what conditions should the system operate? |
| 3. Setup | What opportunity am I looking for? |
| 4. Entry | What triggers execution? |
| 5. Invalidation | What proves the idea wrong? |
| 6. Exit / Management | How should the trade be managed? |
| 7. No-Trade Rules | When should I stay out? |

## Post-trade system assessment

- [ ] Market / instrument was allowed by the system.
- [ ] Market context / regime matched the system rules.
- [ ] Setup was present and valid.
- [ ] Entry followed the defined trigger or confirmation.
- [ ] Invalidation / stop logic matched the system.
- [ ] Trade management followed the plan.
- [ ] Exit followed the defined logic or a valid discretionary rule.
- [ ] No-trade conditions were respected.

## Optional pre-trade system check

When enabled:

- [ ] Market and session are allowed.
- [ ] Context / regime is compatible.
- [ ] Setup is present.
- [ ] Entry trigger or confirmation is present.
- [ ] Invalidation is clear.
- [ ] Exit / target logic is defined.
- [ ] No-trade condition is active: **NO**.

## System evidence

System quality is evaluated over a meaningful sample, not from one trade.

Evidence should progress through:

1. Written rules
2. Annotated valid and invalid examples
3. Backtest sample
4. Forward / simulation sample
5. Live sample
6. Performance by setup, session, market, and regime

## Trading System KPIs

| KPI | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Setup Validity % | Percentage of trades that genuinely met setup rules. |
| Execution Fidelity % | How closely entries, management, and exits followed the system. |
| Expectancy (R) | Average expected R per trade. |
| Profit Factor | Gross wins relative to gross losses. |
| Win Rate | Frequency of winning trades. |
| Average Win / Loss R | Payoff profile. |
| Setup Expectancy | Edge by setup. |
| Regime Expectancy | Edge by market condition. |
| Sample Size | Strength of evidence. |
| System Score | Overall quality of system definition, execution, and evidence. |

# 5. Post-Trade Assessment — Core of the Framework

Every completed trade should answer three questions:

1. **Psychology:** How well did I execute myself?
2. **Risk Management:** How well did I manage risk?
3. **Trading System:** How well did I execute the system?

Then record the trade outcome separately.

## Example

| Area | Result |
|---|---:|
| Trade Result | -1.0R |
| Psychology | 92 / 100 |
| Risk Management | 100 / 100 |
| Trading System Execution | 95 / 100 |
| Process Quality | 96 / 100 |
| Classification | **Good Loss** |

The trade lost money, but the process was strong.

Another trade may produce:

| Area | Result |
|---|---:|
| Trade Result | +3.0R |
| Psychology | 48 / 100 |
| Risk Management | 55 / 100 |
| Trading System Execution | 42 / 100 |
| Process Quality | **FAIL** |
| Classification | **Bad Win** |

The trade made money, but the process was poor.

> **Primary development principle**
>
> Judge the trader by the quality of the process. Judge the system by evidence over a meaningful sample. Treat P&L as an outcome, not as proof of good execution.

---

# 6. Trade Quality Classification

| Process | Outcome | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| Good | Win | **Good Win** |
| Good | Loss | **Good Loss** |
| Good | Breakeven | **Good Breakeven** |
| Bad | Win | **Bad Win** |
| Bad | Loss | **Bad Loss** |
| Bad | Breakeven | **Bad Breakeven** |

> **Important**
>
> **Bad Win** is dangerous because it can reward rule-breaking. **Good Loss** is acceptable because a valid process can still produce a losing outcome.

# 7. Scoring System

The scoring model has two levels:

1. **Trade-Level Assessment** — evaluates how well one trade was executed.
2. **Period-Level Pillar Scores** — evaluates broader development over a rolling sample or review period.

Scores use a simple **0–100 scale**. Hard-rule failures are evaluated separately and can override the numeric score.

### App rating scale

Every trade-level criterion is explicitly recorded as **Pass (100)**, **Partial (50)**, or **Fail (0)**. The journal does not preselect a passing result. A failed criterion requires a reason tag; every partial, failed, or hard-rule review requires one corrective action.

---

## 7.1 Trade-Level Psychology Score

| Sub-score | Weight | How to score |
|---|---:|---|
| Rule Adherence | 35% | Behavioral and execution rules followed. |
| Impulse Control | 25% | No FOMO, chase, revenge, or unplanned trade behavior. |
| Emotional Control | 20% | Emotion did not improperly change the trade. |
| Patience & Discipline | 20% | Waited for valid conditions and respected the plan. |

**Formula:**

`Psychology Trade Score = 0.35 × Rule Adherence + 0.25 × Impulse Control + 0.20 × Emotional Control + 0.20 × Patience & Discipline`

---

## 7.2 Trade-Level Risk Score

| Sub-score | Weight | How to score |
|---|---:|---|
| Risk Policy Adherence | 35% | Actual risk stayed within policy. |
| Position Size Accuracy | 20% | Size matched the intended risk. |
| Stop Discipline | 25% | Invalidation / stop rules were respected. |
| Exposure & Limit Compliance | 20% | Daily, weekly, open-risk, and drawdown rules were respected. |

**Formula:**

`Risk Trade Score = 0.35 × Risk Policy + 0.20 × Position Size + 0.25 × Stop Discipline + 0.20 × Exposure & Limits`

---

## 7.3 Trade-Level Trading System Score

This score evaluates **execution of the system on the specific trade**. It does not include backtest quality or long-term expectancy.

| Sub-score | Weight | How to score |
|---|---:|---|
| Setup Validity | 30% | Trade met the required setup criteria. |
| Context Alignment | 20% | Market regime, session, and conditions were valid. |
| Entry Fidelity | 20% | Entry followed the system. |
| Invalidation / Stop Fidelity | 15% | Stop logic matched the system. |
| Management / Exit Fidelity | 15% | Management and exit followed valid rules. |

**Formula:**

`System Trade Score = 0.30 × Setup Validity + 0.20 × Context Alignment + 0.20 × Entry Fidelity + 0.15 × Invalidation Fidelity + 0.15 × Management/Exit Fidelity`

---

## 7.4 Process Quality Score

If there is **no hard-rule failure**:

`Process Quality = average(Psychology Trade Score, Risk Trade Score, System Trade Score)`

If a hard rule fails:

- Keep the three raw scores for diagnosis.
- Set **Process Quality Status = FAIL**.
- Classify the trade as a process failure even if it was profitable.

---

## 7.5 Period-Level Psychology Score

Use a rolling sample such as the last 20, 30, or 50 trades, or a weekly / monthly review period.

| Sub-score | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Rule Adherence Rate | 35% |
| Impulse Control Rate | 25% |
| Emotional Interference Control | 20% |
| Post-Loss Discipline | 20% |

This score answers:

> **Am I consistently behaving like the trader my plan requires?**

---

## 7.6 Period-Level Risk Management Score

| Sub-score | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Risk Policy Adherence | 35% |
| Stop Discipline | 25% |
| Daily / Weekly / Drawdown Limit Compliance | 25% |
| Exposure Control | 15% |

This score answers:

> **Am I consistently protecting capital according to policy?**

---

## 7.7 Period-Level Trading System Score

The broader System Score combines **execution quality** and **evidence of edge**.

| Sub-score | Weight | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Setup Validity | 20% | Trades match the defined setup. |
| Execution Fidelity | 20% | System rules are executed consistently. |
| Context Alignment | 15% | Trades occur in valid conditions. |
| Evidence Quality | 20% | Sample quality and completeness. |
| Edge Evidence | 25% | Expectancy and related metrics support the system thesis. |

**Formula:**

`Period System Score = 0.20 × Setup Validity + 0.20 × Execution Fidelity + 0.15 × Context Alignment + 0.20 × Evidence Quality + 0.25 × Edge Evidence`

This score answers:

> **Is the system clearly defined, consistently executed, and supported by evidence?**

---

## 7.8 Score Interpretation

| Score | Status | Meaning |
|---:|---|---|
| 90–100 | Strong | Consistent and controlled; maintain and monitor. |
| 80–89 | Good | Reliable with a few improvement areas. |
| 70–79 | Developing | Functional but inconsistent; focused improvement needed. |
| 60–69 | Weak | Material gaps require correction. |
| <60 | Critical | Return to definition, testing, or controlled practice. |

---

## 7.9 Hard-Rule Overrides

A high score cannot erase a critical violation.

| Critical event | Override |
|---|---|
| Daily max-loss breach | Risk = **FAIL** for the review period. |
| Intentional oversized revenge trade | Psychology = **FAIL** and Risk = **FAIL**. |
| Mandatory setup absent | System = **FAIL** for that trade. |
| Stop deliberately widened beyond policy | Risk = **FAIL** for that trade. |
| Trading after a hard shutdown rule | Affected pillar(s) = **FAIL**. |
| Repeated critical violation | Cap affected period-level pillar at 59 until review is completed. |

Hard rules should be configurable to match the trader's actual plan.

In the journal, a hard failure remains active for the selected rolling sample even after its raw numeric score is calculated. A completed weekly or monthly review records the corrective response; it does not rewrite the historical trade assessment.

---

## 7.10 Overall Readiness

Always display the three period-level pillar scores separately.

If one overall number is useful, use a conservative formula:

`Overall Readiness = minimum(Psychology Score, Risk Score, Trading System Score)`

Example:

- Psychology = 88
- Risk = 94
- Trading System = 73
- **Overall Readiness = 73**

The minimum score is intentionally conservative. Two strong pillars should not hide one weak pillar.

The app shows readiness only when its selected rolling sample is complete. Psychology and Trading System are trader-wide; Risk is evaluated for the selected account.

# 8. Diagnosis — Find the Weak Pillar

Before changing the framework, diagnose the source of the problem.

| Psychology | Risk | System | Likely diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak | Good | Good | Execution / behavior problem |
| Good | Weak | Good | Sizing / exposure / control problem |
| Good | Good | Weak | Strategy / edge / regime problem |
| Weak | Weak | Good | Execution and capital-control problem |
| Good | Weak | Weak | Risk and system problem |
| Weak | Good | Weak | Psychology and system problem |
| Weak | Weak | Weak | Return to Define and Test |
| Good | Good | Good | Do not overreact to short-term P&L variance |

The purpose of diagnosis is to improve the weak area **without unnecessarily changing the strong areas**.

# 9. Review Cycle

## After each trade — Required

Focus on:

- Result in R
- Psychology execution
- Risk execution
- Trading System execution
- Hard-rule violations
- Good Win / Good Loss / Bad Win / Bad Loss classification
- Main lesson when relevant

## Daily — Optional / lightweight

Focus on:

- Major behavior events
- Risk limits
- Repeated mistakes
- Mental state when useful

## Weekly — Recommended

Focus on:

- Psychology patterns
- Risk violations
- Setup quality
- Rule adherence
- Repeated mistakes
- R performance
- One priority for improvement

## Monthly / Sample-Based — Required for framework development

Focus on:

- Psychology trend
- Risk trend
- System expectancy
- Drawdown
- Setup performance
- Regime performance
- Critical violations
- Framework changes

## Strategy Review

Review strategy rules only after a meaningful sample or a predefined review point. Avoid changing the system because of a few recent wins or losses.

---

# 10. Improvement Loop

The framework should create a continuous development cycle:

`Trade → Assess → Measure → Diagnose → Improve → Retest`

Use this simple improvement process:

1. **Identify** the weakest pillar or recurring problem.
2. **Find** the specific behavior, rule, or condition causing it.
3. **Choose one priority** to improve.
4. **Define a corrective action or hypothesis.**
5. **Collect a new sample.**
6. **Compare results.**
7. **Keep, revise, or reject the change.**

Avoid changing multiple system variables at once unless necessary.

---

# 11. Maturity Roadmap

The same five maturity levels apply to all three pillars.

| Level | Goal | Gate idea |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Define | Rules are clear. | Rules, limits, examples, and definitions documented. |
| 2. Test | Rules are exercised. | Representative sample and no major ambiguity. |
| 3. Execute | Rules are followed. | Adherence threshold met; critical violations controlled. |
| 4. Measure | Evidence supports diagnosis. | Rolling metrics and review cadence available. |
| 5. Optimize | Changes are evidence-driven. | Hypothesis, test, result, keep/reject decision documented. |

Each pillar can be at a different maturity level.

Example:

- Psychology — Level 3: Execute
- Risk Management — Level 4: Measure
- Trading System — Level 2: Test

This is more useful than assigning one generic trader level.

# 12. Minimum App Implementation

Keep the app simple enough that post-trade assessment actually gets completed.

| Module | Minimum data |
|---|---|
| Trade | Instrument, direction, entry, exit, result in R, date/time |
| Psychology Assessment | Rule adherence, FOMO/chase/revenge flags, emotional interference, violations |
| Risk Assessment | Planned risk, actual risk, stop behavior, daily/open risk, breaches |
| System Assessment | Setup, context/regime, entry fidelity, invalidation, management/exit fidelity, system version |
| Trade Review | Hard-rule status, lesson, trade quality classification |
| Period Review | Three pillar scores, repeated issues, strongest/weakest pillar, priority action |
| Dashboard | Pillar trends, Process Quality, R performance, violations, readiness |

## Optional app module: Pre-Trade Check

Allow traders to enable or disable pre-trade checks.

If enabled, the module may include:

- Mental readiness
- Risk capacity
- Setup validation
- Planned entry / stop / target

But the absence of a pre-trade record should **not prevent post-trade assessment**.

> **Keep it simple**
>
> Capture only data that improves scoring, diagnosis, or decision quality. Add complexity when the evidence shows it is useful.

---

# 13. Framework Summary

- **Psychology** measures how well the trader executes behaviorally.
- **Risk Management** measures how well capital and exposure are controlled.
- **Trading System** measures whether trades follow a defined system and whether that system is supported by evidence.
- **Post-trade assessment is the core of the framework.**
- **Pre-trade checks are optional.**
- Trade outcome and trade quality are always separate.
- Hard-rule failures override soft scores.
- Per-trade scores measure execution quality.
- Period-level scores measure development and consistency.
- Reviews identify the weak pillar before changes are made.
- The purpose of the framework is continuous, evidence-based trader improvement.

> **Final principle**
>
> **Edge × Risk Control × Execution.** The framework is strong only when all three pillars are strong enough to work together.
